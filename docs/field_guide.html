short history, or windows cross a holiday transitionFix the window first

Get more history, move the test out of Q4, or keep before/after windows comparable. Rankings and MDE from short holiday data don't generalize.

  • National change between before/after windows
  • GeoLift warning “Small pre-treatment period”
Results · cases 05, 08
  • 64 days vs 112 recommended
  • 32-day test MDE 8%, worse than 28-day 6% (crossed early-Nov lull)
Q2

Which geos get the treatment?

Work through the three steps in order.

Step 1 · exclusion rulesDrop tiny geos; keep giant geos as control only

Median < 10 / day → too noisy. Share > 15% → too expensive, weakens the control, no counterfactual.

Step 2 · defaultStratified randomization

Sort by size, split into strata, pick one geo per stratum. Same noise as random, predictable budget.

Step 3 · optionalSearch for the “best” set

Only with long, representative history, and always check the ranking on a held-out period.

Look at
  • Budget share (p10–p90 across draws)
  • Backtest RMSE (history)
  • Correlation of backtest vs held-out error: ≈ 0 means don't trust the ranking
Results · cases 06, 08
  • Dropped 17 tiny states; California (21%) control only
  • Budget share stratified 12–16% vs random 8–19%
  • Top-10 searched sets 4× better in history, correlation with January −0.01
  • GeoLift #1 MDE 2.5%, but #2–#5 worse than random in January
Q3

Can this design detect the effect you expect?

Simulate fake tests on history: inject a known lift into random geos, re-estimate hundreds of times, count how often it clears the noise line.

expected effect > MDELaunch
expected effect ≈ MDEBorderline (~80% chance)

Add a safety margin before committing budget.

expected effect < MDERedesign or don't run

Raise spend intensity, add geos (helps ~1/√n, diminishing), use a more stable KPI, avoid Q4. An underpowered test wastes budget and gets misread as “ads don't work”.

Look at
  • Power curve; MDE = smallest lift with power ≥ 80%
  • α = 0.05
  • Sanity: power at 0% ≈ 5%
Results · case 05
  • 6 states × 28 days: MDE 6%
  • 3 / 6 / 10 / 15 states: MDE 8 / 6 / ≈4.5 / 4%
  • Expected 2%: power 0.14; even 17 states only 0.42 → don't run

Q4

Which estimator builds the counterfactual?

before vs after, treated geo onlyNaive lift: never use alone

Credits seasonality to the ads.

pre-trends roughly parallelDiD

Simple, explainable. Treated change ÷ control change − 1.

treated unit unlike any simple controlSynthetic control

Weighted mix of donors (w ≥ 0, Σw = 1) fit on the pre-period.

treated unit outside the donor rangeAugmented SCM (GeoLift, ridge)

Allows negative weights to extrapolate. Check for one dominant donor.

Look at
  • Indexed pre-period plot / correlation (parallel?)
  • Pre-period MAE or scaled L2 imbalance (fit)
  • Weights: sparse? negative? one donor dominating?
  • Placebo error across all units, the deciding number
Results · cases 01, 03, 08
  • Naive Texas −3.4% with true effect 0
  • DiD Texas −0.7%; typical single state 5.1%; 6 pooled 1.8%
  • SCM fit MAE 0.067, placebo 3.3% vs DiD 2.9% (17 of 34)
  • GeoLift 7.4% vs DiD 6.9% (truth 8%); placebo weight California 1.08

Rule: choose the method by placebo error, not by pre-period fit or by how sophisticated it is. Better fit is training error; placebo error is test error.

Q5

Is the lift real or noise?

Placebo test: draw fake treated groups from control geos only, on data with no effect, ~1,000 times.

p < 0.05 and CI excludes 0Real effect

Report the estimate with its CI.

p ≈ 0.05, or CI barely touches 0Borderline

Report the range; don't lean on the word “significant”.

p ≥ 0.05Can't tell from noise

Means “underpowered”, not “no effect”. Go back to Q3.

Look at
  • p-value = share of |placebo| ≥ |estimate| (two-sided)
  • 95% CI = estimate − 97.5th / 2.5th percentile of placebos
  • With B placebos, report p < 1/B, never p = 0
Results · cases 04, 08
  • True 8%: estimate 6.9%, p < 0.001, CI [2.8%, 10.5%]
  • True 2%: p 0.76 (real effect missed)
  • True 5%: p 0.04 but CI [−0.1%, 7.6%]
  • GeoLift placebo: +4.5%, p 0.61
DecideMoney
Q6

Was it worth the money?

Lift → incremental sessions → incremental revenue (× CVR × AOV) → iROAS. Compare the iROAS CI with the profit break-even, 1 ÷ margin.

whole iROAS CI above 1 / marginScale
CI straddles 1 / marginHold and re-test

Try a lower spend level; returns usually diminish with spend.

whole CI below 1 / marginCut or reduce

The ads work but lose money at this spend.

Look at
  • iROAS and its range (from the lift CI)
  • Profit = incremental revenue × margin − spend
  • Platform ROAS vs iROAS → incrementality factor → calibrate platform numbers and MMM
Results · case 07
  • Incremental sessions 716, revenue ≈ $1,066, spend $800
  • iROAS 1.33 (0.57–1.97) < break-even 2.0 → loses money in every case
  • Platform ROAS 3.5 → only 38% incremental

Rule: “the ads work” and “the ads are worth it” are different questions. Answer both.
